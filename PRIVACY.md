# Daysplit privacy policy

_Last updated: October 8, 2026_

Daysplit is a browser extension that shows how much time you spend on each website. It is built to work entirely inside your browser. This policy explains what it handles and where that data goes.

## The short version

Daysplit keeps everything on your device. It has no servers, no accounts, no analytics and no ads, and it never sends your data anywhere.

## What Daysplit records

- **Website domains and time.** To know which site you're using, Daysplit reads the address of the active tab and keeps only its domain, for example `github.com`. It saves how long you spent on each domain, in one-hour blocks.
- **Your categories and settings.** These are the categories you choose for sites (such as "youtube.com is Entertainment") and your preferences, such as the idle timeout.

Daysplit does **not** record full page addresses, page titles, page contents, form input, keystrokes or anything you type. It doesn't run any code inside the pages you visit. Private and incognito windows are never tracked.

## Where it's stored

All of this is saved in your browser's local extension storage on your computer. It's not synced to other devices and not sent to the developer or anyone else.

## How long it's kept

History is kept for 180 days by default. You can change this to 30 days, 90 days, a year or forever in **Rules & settings**. You can also erase everything there at any time. Uninstalling the extension deletes all of its data.

## Export

If you click **Export JSON**, Daysplit creates a file with your history, rules and settings and saves it where you choose. Nothing is uploaded.

## Permissions and why they're needed

| Permission | Why |
|---|---|
| Tabs | To read the active tab's address so the time can be credited to the right domain. |
| Storage, unlimited storage | To save your history, categories and settings on your device. Unlimited storage (Chromium only) keeps long histories from hitting the browser's 10 MB default limit. |
| Idle | To pause counting when you step away from your computer. |
| Alarms | For a once-a-minute heartbeat that saves time accurately, and to schedule the daily summary. |
| Notifications | To show the optional end-of-day summary. |

## Third parties

Daysplit shares no data with anyone. It doesn't sell data, use it for advertising, or use it for anything other than showing you your own browsing time.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted in this file in the [Daysplit repository](https://github.com/ShaifArfan/daysplit). Its history shows every change.
